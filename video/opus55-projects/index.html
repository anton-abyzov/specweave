Chapter five:
what keeps a project moving.
Routines are agents that run on a
schedule or on a trigger.
Here are the built-in templates.
A morning briefing,
email triage,
a system health check,
issue triage.
Pull request digests,
dependency checks,
and release notes every time something merges.
And here's what happens when you hit
the weekly limit.
The whole project pauses,
and Claude tells you exactly when it
will pick everything back up on its
own.
Auto-continue restarts every thread once
the limit resets.
That helps.
But you can't always wait for a
reset.
So what if you need to keep
going right now?
Chapter six.
You hand off.
This is SpecWeave,
my open-source tool.
It keeps the work itself in git:
the spec,
the tasks,
who's on what,
and the next step.
I've set it to hand off by
itself at ninety percent of the limit.
When I'm close to the limit,
I just say hand off,
or it fires on its own.
It releases my task,
writes down where things stand,
and pushes my branch,
including uncommitted edits.
Then in another tool,
on another machine or account,
I say:
pick up.
And it comes back with the same
spec,
the same task,
the next step and its acceptance criteria.
Right where I left off.
It's free.
To install it,
run npm install,
-g specweave.
The command is in the description.
